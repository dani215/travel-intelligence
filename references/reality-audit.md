# Reality Audit Protocol

Use this reference before final synthesis. The audit is a decision gate, not a decorative section heading.

## 1. Identify what can be wrong

List the material claims behind the recommendation. Mark claims that are time-sensitive, location-specific, financial, safety-related, accessibility-related, language-specific, or based on “locals say”. Check the user's dates, party size, mobility, baggage, budget, starting point, and tolerance for complexity.

## 2. Match verification depth to risk

| Risk | Required audit |
|---|---|
| Low: general orientation or stable background | Check internal consistency and avoid presenting opinion as fact |
| Medium: venues, food prices, opening hours, local customs, transit | Use current official or local sources and record checked time |
| High: flights, bookings, money, safety, legal rules, medical/accessibility needs | Verify current details from primary sources where possible, cross-check conflicts, and show assumptions and failure modes |

If browsing or source access is unavailable, say so and mark unstable claims as indicative or unverified. Do not simulate live research.

## 3. Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order: official operators and government sources; airline or venue pages; local-language municipal and local-media sources; current menus and business listings; resident discussions and local creators; aggregators and summaries. Use lower-tier sources to discover leads, not to overrule stronger current evidence without explaining why.

## 4. Separate evidence classes

- **Confirmed:** directly supported by a current, relevant source.
- **Corroborated:** supported by two independent relevant sources.
- **Indicative:** plausible but only partially verified or variable by date/venue.
- **Inference:** reasoned conclusion; label it as such.
- **Unknown:** evidence is missing or conflicting; qualify or remove the claim.

Use this compact ledger when the decision is material:

| Claim | Source type | Checked at | Status | Confidence | Final treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| … | official/local/anecdotal/unavailable | date and time | confirmed/corroborated/indicative/inference/unknown | high/medium/low | state/use/qualify/remove |

## 5. Resolve conflicts

Compare publication or update dates, geographic fit, primary-source status, exactness, and whether the source describes a variable condition. If a conflict remains material, show both values and explain which one drives the recommendation. Do not average contradictory prices or invent a compromise timetable.

## 6. Mode-specific checks

- **Flights:** dates, airports, passenger count, fare family, bags, seats, fees, self-transfer, minimum buffer, ground transport, checkout status, and door-to-door arithmetic.
- **Transit:** service date, first/last departure, ticket validity, zones, accessibility, disruptions, airport supplement, walking segments, and return feasibility.
- **Food:** current opening status, menu or price evidence, local dish identification, dietary constraints, reservation/payment rules, and whether the venue is genuinely local or merely marketed as such.
- **Local places:** evidence of local relevance, access rules, seasonality, crowd impact, respectful behavior, and a lower-impact alternative.
- **Language:** actual regional language, register, pronunciation limits, gender/formality, and whether a phrase is natural in that location rather than merely grammatical.

## 7. Final gate

Before answering, confirm: the recommendation is direct; material inputs are explicit; arithmetic is correct; unstable facts have a checked-at boundary; source types are clear; unsupported claims are removed or qualified; conflicts and risks are visible; and the final version is the most useful verified answer for the user's actual constraints.
